Start with an X-ray
Industries · Insurance

The claims department reads. By hand.

Tens of thousands of new claims a year. Each one opened, sorted, linked, checked for missing documents, read, summarized and answered in a letter. Most of that time is reading.

Where the time goes

Six stations, one of them eats the day.

Email intake and sorting. Linking to a claim. Opening the claim and sorting documents. The missing-document check. Reading the case and summarizing it. Drafting the letter.

In one agency, reading the medical documents alone came to about 44 hours a day across the department. That is the potential we found in two days on site at that agency. It is an estimate from team interviews until the agency's own data confirms it, and we say so.

The first processes

Where we start.

01

Case summaries.

A structured medical summary per case, by policy chapter, that the adjuster reads before opening the file and marks useful or wrong.

02

Completion letters.

The adjuster picks a letter type. The draft comes with the details filled in from the file. The adjuster approves.

03

Readable or not.

First, a report of which document types the system can read automatically, on real files, with nothing leaving your tenant.

Red lines

What the agent never does.

Never approves or rejects a claim.

A person decides. Always.

Never writes to your core system.

Read-only on the policy and claims systems.

Never replies to the insured on its own.

Every letter is approved before it goes out.

Sensitive cases stay fully manual.

You name them before we start. Oncology and life-saving drugs, for example.
