The way in

One day on site. Six stations. One ranked list.

An X-ray is not a workshop and not a questionnaire. It is a day with the people who do the work, and a map of the process the way it really runs, not the way the manual says.

Each station gets a time. The time becomes hours a year. The hours become a ranked list of what to fix first, by most time saved for least complexity.

The build

Written down before the first line of code.

01

Success bar.

The number that has to move, and by how much. Reading time 40 percent shorter on a large case. A minute and a half saved per letter.

02

Stop rule.

When we pause, in writing. Three factual errors in twenty cases, for example.

03

Red lines.

What the agent never does. Never approves a claim. Never writes to the ERP. Never replies to a customer on its own.

The measure

A number you already watch.

Hours in a department. Days per building. The sales line. A plan that matches the manager's. We do not invent a KPI; we take the one the owner already looks at every week, and we move it. Part of our fee rides on it; some owners still choose a fixed fee, and that is fine.

Estimates are labeled estimates until the data confirms them. A potential found is not a saving delivered, and we say which is which.

Nobody walked into your business to understand how it really works. We do.
